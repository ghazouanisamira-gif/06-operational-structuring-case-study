# Team Performance, Coaching & Capability Development

## Purpose

This workstream demonstrates a practical **people-and-performance enablement approach**: translating operational observations into targeted coaching, role-specific support, practical tools, structured routines and measurable capability development.

The objective was not simply to "train people", but to help employees and operational managers **understand expectations, organize their work, apply the right methods and tools, solve recurring problems and progressively operate with greater autonomy**.

## Scope

The approach covered two complementary populations:

### 1. Quality / Support / Operational Contributors

Support included:

- technical and operational coaching
- methodology and process coaching
- tool adoption and practical usage
- quality standards and evaluation practices
- feedback and corrective coaching
- calibration and consistency
- problem-solving approaches
- prioritization and organization
- knowledge-base usage
- follow-up and continuous improvement
- development of autonomy and ownership

### 2. Production / Operational Managers

Manager enablement included:

- onboarding support
- clarification of role, responsibilities and interfaces
- understanding of operational expectations and KPIs
- structuring the manager's daily and weekly routines
- prioritization of recurring activities
- creation or adaptation of practical To-Do Lists / management checklists
- selection of appropriate tools and working methods
- coaching on team follow-up and performance routines
- support on reporting and action tracking
- targeted training according to identified capability gaps
- follow-up during the transition toward autonomy

## Manager Onboarding Approach

Manager onboarding was treated as an **operational enablement process**, not only an information-transfer exercise.

The approach could include:

**Understand the role → Assess needs → Clarify priorities → Select tools → Build routines → Coach application → Observe → Adjust → Reinforce autonomy**

Key onboarding dimensions:

| Dimension | Support provided |
|---|---|
| Role clarity | Responsibilities, interfaces, expectations and escalation points |
| Operational understanding | Processes, customer journey, standards and key dependencies |
| Performance management | KPIs, dashboard interpretation, review routines and action follow-up |
| Daily organization | Priorities, recurring tasks, time allocation and management checklist |
| Tools | Selection, configuration guidance and practical adoption |
| Team management | Follow-up, feedback, coaching and issue escalation |
| Reporting | What to report, when, to whom and with what level of analysis |
| Problem solving | RCA, structured diagnosis and corrective action |
| Capability development | Targeted training/coaching based on observed needs |
| Autonomy | Progressive reduction of support as routines become stable |

## Designing the Right Daily Management Routine

A key principle was that **the tool should fit the role**, rather than forcing every manager into the same system.

The routine could be structured around:

- critical daily controls
- team follow-up
- priority actions
- customer / operational issues
- KPI review
- coaching actions
- reporting deadlines
- cross-functional coordination
- escalations
- improvement actions

The resulting To-Do List or management checklist was therefore treated as a **management control mechanism**, not merely a personal task list.

## Coaching Methodology

Coaching followed a structured cycle:

**Observe → Diagnose → Discuss → Demonstrate → Practice → Feedback → Apply → Follow Up → Reassess**

### Observe

Use available evidence:

- KPI trends
- quality observations
- process adherence
- dashboard signals
- customer journey drop-offs
- reporting
- feedback
- recurring operational issues

### Diagnose

Identify the underlying capability or operating gap:

- knowledge gap
- technical skill gap
- methodological gap
- tool-usage gap
- process misunderstanding
- prioritization issue
- communication / stakeholder-management need
- unclear responsibility
- insufficient management routine
- systemic process constraint

This distinction is important: **not every performance gap is a training problem**.

### Coach

Adapt the intervention to the actual cause:

- explanation
- demonstration
- guided practice
- role play / simulation where relevant
- job aid
- checklist
- knowledge-base reference
- targeted training
- feedback conversation
- process clarification
- tool coaching
- management routine redesign

### Follow Up

Verify whether the intervention translated into changed behaviour or improved execution.

Possible evidence:

- application of the method
- improved process adherence
- greater autonomy
- reduction of recurring errors
- improved KPI trend
- better reporting quality
- completion of agreed actions

## Capability Development Model

Capability development was approached across four layers:

**Knowledge → Skill → Application → Autonomy**

Training creates knowledge, but operational performance depends on the ability to apply that knowledge consistently in the real work environment.

The development cycle therefore connected:

**Need → Learning intervention → Practical application → Observation → Feedback → Reinforcement → Reassessment**

## Individual Development

Where appropriate, support could be individualized through:

- identified strengths
- development gaps
- agreed improvement objectives
- coaching actions
- practice opportunities
- follow-up checkpoints
- evidence of progress
- next development priorities

The focus was on **specific, observable behaviours and capabilities**, rather than generic training attendance.

## Manager Enablement Principles

The approach was built around several management principles:

1. **Clarify before correcting** — ensure the person understands the expected outcome.
2. **Diagnose before training** — determine whether the gap is knowledge, skill, process, tool, organization or system-related.
3. **Make tools practical** — use the simplest tool that supports the required behaviour.
4. **Adapt to the role** — routines should reflect the manager's actual responsibilities.
5. **Coach toward autonomy** — the objective is not permanent dependency on the coach.
6. **Use evidence** — coaching should be connected to observable facts whenever possible.
7. **Close the loop** — every intervention should have follow-up.
8. **Separate individual from systemic causes** — do not attribute a process problem to an individual capability gap.
9. **Reinforce good practice** — development is not limited to correcting weaknesses.
10. **Embed learning into the operating model** — successful practices should become documented, reusable and transferable.

## Connection to Continuous Improvement

People development was integrated into the improvement cycle:

**Performance signal → Root-cause analysis → Capability / process diagnosis → Intervention → Application → Measurement → Standardization → Continuous improvement**

When a recurring gap was identified, the response was not automatically "train the person". The analysis considered:

- People
- Process
- Tools
- Information
- Governance
- Workload / organization
- Measurement
- Environment

This supports a more mature **management-system approach to performance improvement**.
